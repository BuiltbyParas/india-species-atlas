# Voice script: the 62-second Atlas film

**Status:** script for approval. Supersedes `reel/audio/voice/VOICE_SCRIPT.md` (the 18-line draft).
**Speaker:** the Atlas guide (`CHARACTER_DESIGN.md`).
**Performer:** a real person. **No AI or TTS voice is used anywhere, including as a scratch track.** Until the
recording exists, the cut shows each line as a caption at its planned time and plays no voice.

## 1. The writing in brief

- **14 lines, 99 words, about 23 s of speech in 62 s.** Nearly two-thirds of the film has no voice. Signal, the landscape
  montage, three of the five species, the data sweep, the fragment, the three questions and the wordmark all
  play without it.
- **Trailer, not narration.** She almost never describes what is on screen. She reacts to it, sets it up, or
  adds the one thing the picture can't say (the dolphin is nearly blind; a power line kills birds).
- **On-screen text is not read aloud.** "One country. Many landscapes.", "Life, mapped." and the three
  questions are left to the type. The voice sets them up or answers them. There is one deliberate exception:
  the first closing line (V13), which the voice says and the screen completes.

## 2. The performance

A young adult guide in the manner of a good anime dub: a real person talking to one friend, not announcing.

- **Register:** natural mid-range. Never pitched up, and no "anime squeak" or cartoon brightness.
- **Default:** intelligent, curious, warm, slightly energetic, with a smile you can hear but don't see.
- **Threats (V07–V09):** the energy drops but the volume doesn't. Plain and serious, never sad-voiced.
- **The ending (V13–V14):** quiet confidence, then the warmest line in the film, said to someone close.
- **Keep:** breaths, small mouth sounds, the odd imperfect consonant. Don't clean them out.
- **Avoid:** YouTube-explainer lilt, trailer-voice growl, a salesy brightness on the brand name, upspeak.

Pause marks used below: `/` a beat (~0.2 s) · `//` a breath (~0.5 s) · `…` a held, unfinished pause (~0.4 s).

## 3. Lines

Times are **planned**. When the takes arrive, each line's real length is measured and **the picture moves to fit
the voice**. The voice is never time-stretched.

### V01 · 0:04.1 · 0.8 s
**LINE:** "Look closer."
**EMOTION:** Curious, intimate.
**DELIVERY:** Barely above a murmur, as if she has just noticed the map drawing itself. A small lift on "clo-".
**PAUSE:** 1.4 s after (the coastline keeps drawing).
**CHARACTER ACTION:** C1. Small full figure, back three-quarter, looking up; her head follows the pen.
**VISUAL BEAT:** P02. The coastline traces from the ruler; the graticule fades up.

### V02 · 0:06.3 · 2.1 s
**LINE:** "One country. / So many worlds inside it."
**EMOTION:** Wonder, with warmth.
**DELIVERY:** "One country" lands exactly with the on-screen "One country." (a deliberate echo). Then she goes her
own way: open and unhurried on "worlds".
**PAUSE:** 7.6 s. The dive and the landscape montage play without voice.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** None (C1 has left; the camera moved past her).
**VISUAL BEAT:** P03. Fill, borders and relief activate; "One country. / Many landscapes." masks in.

### V03 · 0:16.0 · 1.6 s
**LINE:** "And each one shapes / what can live there."
**EMOTION:** Thoughtful.
**DELIVERY:** Calm, explaining to herself as much as to us. A slight fall on "there", left a little open.
**PAUSE:** 0.0 s. V04 follows almost directly, as a turn.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** Voice over the picture, off screen.
**VISUAL BEAT:** P08. The Himalaya window: F05's ridge, then the high contours glow.
**NOTE:** The landscape montage (P04–P07, 7 s) has **no voice**. Only ambience, the window clicks and the
rhythm.

### V04 · 0:17.6 · 0.8 s
**LINE:** "So… / who does?"
**EMOTION:** Playful, curious.
**DELIVERY:** The film's one bit of play. "So…" with a small smile, then "who does?" brighter, as if about to
introduce someone she likes.
**PAUSE:** 0.6 s.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** Off screen.
**VISUAL BEAT:** P09. "Life, mapped." types in; the camera rises to the whole country.

### V05 · 0:19.0 · 1.7 s
**LINE:** "One of the heaviest flying birds / in the world."
**EMOTION:** Admiring, a quiet "look at that".
**DELIVERY:** Even pace; a little weight on "heaviest". Not a fact being recited: something she likes about it.
**PAUSE:** 2.7 s. The tiger card plays without voice: let the tiger be seen.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** Off screen.
**VISUAL BEAT:** P10. The bustard card: line wipe, push forward, Rajasthan and Gujarat fill, CR chip.

### V06 · 0:23.4 · 2.0 s
**LINE:** "Almost blind. // It finds its way… / by sound."
**EMOTION:** Tender, fascinated.
**DELIVERY:** Softer and closer than anything before. "Almost blind." is plain, then a breath. "by sound" is almost

**PAUSE:** 8.3 s. The rhino and snow leopard cards and the data sweep play without voice.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** C5. Profile at the left edge; she glances at the photo, tilts her head, and her eyes close on

**VISUAL BEAT:** P12. The dolphin card; the Ganga and Brahmaputra states fill; the link-line ripple.
**CLAIM:** `ganges-river-dolphin.summary`: "an almost-blind dolphin that navigates … by echolocation".

### V07 · 0:33.7 · 0.9 s
**LINE:** "Now look again."
**EMOTION:** Serious, the turn of the film.
**DELIVERY:** Lower, slower, with no smile. A callback to V01 ("Look closer."), so the same shape but with the warmth

**PAUSE:** 1.3 s.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** Off screen.
**VISUAL BEAT:** P16. A hard cut into the Thar; the bustard's states glow; BUSTARD → POWER LINES.

### V08 · 0:35.9 · 1.8 s
**LINE:** "Sometimes, / the threat is literally / a line."
**EMOTION:** Serious, plain. **The key line of the film.**
**DELIVERY:** Unhurried. "literally" is not jokey; it is matter-of-fact. Slow down on "a line", and let it land and

**PAUSE:** 1.1 s. The hum carries it, then cuts dead on the freeze.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** Off screen through the line; she appears in C3 just after it.
**VISUAL BEAT:** P17. The F02 window: a transmission line over the same grassland; the threat sentence is quoted on

**CLAIM:** `great-indian-bustard.threatNote`: "Collision with high-tension power lines crossing the Thar is now the

### V09 · 0:38.8 · 2.6 s
**LINE:** "Roads cut through the tiger's forests… / and the corridors between them."
**EMOTION:** Concern, controlled.
**DELIVERY:** The first half travels with the camera. Hold "forests…" open, then drop to the end, falling on

**PAUSE:** 1.8 s. The fragment plays without voice.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** Off screen; C4 (her fingertip on the breaking line) follows at 0:40.8.

**LINE:** "To protect a species, / you have to know / three things."

**CHARACTER ACTION:** Off screen.

### V11 · 0:48.6 · 1.4 s
**LINE:** "So we put all three / on one map."
**EMOTION:** Quiet pride, a little energy.
**DELIVERY:** Forward and simple. "one map" is clear, not punched.
**PAUSE:** 0.5 s.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** Off screen.
**VISUAL BEAT:** P22. The map registers onto the real site's map; the interface frame draws round it.

### V12 · 0:50.5 · 1.6 s
**LINE:** "Tap a pin… / and the whole story opens."
**EMOTION:** Inviting, bright.
**DELIVERY:** "Tap a pin…" goes with the tap; then "the whole story opens" opens up, with a smile.
**PAUSE:** 3.7 s. The tabs and layers play on UI ticks and music.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** C6. Her hand taps the pin on "Tap".
**VISUAL BEAT:** P23. The pin press opens the profile; then P24, the tabs and layers.

### V13 · 0:55.8 · 2.0 s
**LINE:** "Mapping isn't just about / where species are."
**EMOTION:** Reflective, sure.
**DELIVERY:** Slower than everything since V08. "where species are" lands down, unresolved. The screen finishes the
thought ("It is about where we choose to protect them.") **in silence**.
**PAUSE:** 0.8 s.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** Off screen.
**VISUAL BEAT:** P25. Out of the interface over the whole map with every layer on; the closing lines.
**SOURCE:** the site's own closing line (`src/components/cinema/ConservationMap.tsx`). The text stays verbatim
("is not"). The voice uses the natural contraction.

### V14 · 0:58.6 · 1.4 s
**LINE:** "Come and see / where they live."
**EMOTION:** Warm, direct. The last word of the film.
**DELIVERY:** To camera, close-mic, as if to one person beside her. A small smile on "live". Don't push the volume;
pull back slightly. The music has already gone.
**PAUSE:** To the end. The wordmark arrives in silence, then the final impact.
**CHARACTER ACTION:** C7. She turns from the outline to camera (CHAR_07), says the line, and settles into the warm
look (CHAR_08). The camera tilts up off her.
**VISUAL BEAT:** P26. Every layer has fallen away except the outline. Then the wordmark, rule, tagline and URL.

## 6. Recording guide

1. **Deliver:** `reel/audio/voice/character_voice.wav`, one continuous top-to-bottom read; plus `V01.wav` …
   `V14.wav` and the alternates, each with at least three takes (as written, a touch bigger, a touch smaller).
   48 kHz, 24-bit, mono. Each file starts and ends with 0.5 s of room tone, and there are 10 s of room tone on
   its own.
2. **Room:** a treated small room or a wardrobe full of clothes. No hard walls within a metre.
3. **Mic:** a large-diaphragm condenser or an SM7B-type dynamic, a pop filter, 15–20 cm away, slightly off axis.
   **V06 and V14 are recorded closer (8–10 cm)** for intimacy.
4. **Level:** peaks around −10 dBFS, with no compressor or limiter on the way in.
5. **Watch-along:** the actor watches the timing cut (step 1 of the production order) while recording, so they see
   what each line sits on.
6. **Processing (afterwards only):** high-pass at 80 Hz → subtractive EQ (dip any 250–400 Hz boxiness) →
   de-ess only where needed ("species", "sometimes") → 2:1 compression with 3–4 dB of gain reduction → barely
   audible saturation → a short plate at 5–7 % wet on the map shots only, dry on V06 and V14. The dialogue stem
   targets −16 LUFS. **No pitch or formant shifting, and no "anime" effect.** The performance is the effect.
